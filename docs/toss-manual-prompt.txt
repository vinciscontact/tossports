HOW TO USE IT

You are writing two manuals for Toss Sports, a handmade cricket bat business in
Chennai that sells at tossports.com. I want them to read like good instruction
books — the kind someone actually finishes — not like software documentation.

says, and how it behaves:

  https://tossports.com/#/finder        "Find My Bat" — the guided questions
  https://tossports.com/#/game          "Play & Win" — the cricket game

  https://tossports.com/refund-policy/    refunds
  https://tossports.com/shipping-policy/  shipping
Pick up the real colours (deep navy and orange), the typefaces, the tone of
voice, the words the site itself uses for things, and the actual prices and
policies. Use those words in the books. Do not invent features, prices,
timelines or policies: if something is not on the site and not in the appendices
below, either leave it out or mark it clearly as "[TO CONFIRM]".

WHO IS READING
  Book 1: the two founders, plus managers, sales staff and workshop staff.
          Some are very comfortable with phones and computers; some are not.
          Several will read it on a phone. Some may prefer plain English over
          jargon, and may be reading in their second language.
  Book 2: customers buying a cricket bat — mostly players and club organisers
          across India, often on a phone, often buying a bat online for the
          first time. Also useful for stakeholders and partners who want to
          understand what the business offers.

HOUSE STYLE FOR BOTH BOOKS
  - Short sentences. Everyday words. Explain any term the first time it appears.
  - Second person: "you". Never "the user".
  - Every instruction is a numbered step that starts with a verb, and says what
    you should see on screen after the step: "Tap Products. The list of all 31
    bats opens."
  - Each chapter opens with one line saying what it covers and who needs it.
  - Use tables for anything comparative (roles, statuses, prices, policies).
  - Add a "If something goes wrong" box at the end of each chapter: the two or
    three things most likely to confuse someone, and what to do.
  - Describe where things are on screen in words ("the row of icons down the
    left edge"), since you cannot take screenshots of the Maze Room.
  - Where a screenshot would help, insert a clearly marked placeholder:
        [SCREENSHOT: Products list showing the Mark out of stock button]
    and gather them into a checklist at the end so the team can take them.
  - Mark anything dangerous or irreversible with a warning line.
  - Design it to match the site: navy and orange, generous headings, plenty of
    white space, page numbers, and a table of contents with page numbers.

BOOK 1 — THE MAZE ROOM HANDBOOK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Maze Room is the private admin panel at https://tossports.com/maze.html.
Only staff can sign in. It runs the whole business: orders, stock, billing,
staff, money and the website's own content.

Structure it like this:

  Part 1 — Before you start
    What the Maze Room is, and the one rule: it is the real business, not a
    practice copy. Anything changed here shows on the public shop within
    seconds. Signing in, signing out, forgetting a password, and what to do if
    the screen says "Your role doesn't have access to this".

  Part 2 — Your role, and what you can see
    Explain the five roles plainly, using Appendix A. Give the full access
    table. Explain that the sections a person cannot open are not merely hidden
    — the database itself refuses them, so nothing can be reached by guessing a
    web address. Explain the founder-only group and why those six sections
    (team, money, history, branches, settings, insights) are locked down.

  Part 3 — Day-to-day work, one chapter per section
    Write a chapter for each of the nineteen sections listed in Appendix A.
    In each chapter: what the section is for, who can open it, the most common
    job done there step by step, and what NOT to do. Give the everyday jobs the
    most room — taking an order through to delivered, adding or editing a bat,
    marking a bat out of stock, raising a bill, answering an enquiry, setting a
    task. Keep the rarely-used sections short.

  Part 4 — The jobs that cross several sections
    Walk these end to end, because this is how work actually happens:
      - A new online order arrives, and what happens to it until it is delivered
      - A bat is out of stock, and how the shop shows that (Appendix C)
      - A customer wants a repair, a custom bat, or a jersey order
      - A warranty claim
      - A refund
      - Adding a new member of staff and giving them access
      - Month end: what a founder checks

  Part 5 — Looking after it
    Who to call when something breaks. What is backed up and what is not.
    The handful of settings that change the public website, and the care they
    need. A one-page "first week" checklist for a new staff member.

BOOK 2 — THE TOSS SPORTS GUIDE (for customers)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This one should feel like part of the brand, not a manual: warm, confident,
proud of the fact that every bat is shaped by hand in Chennai and never resold.
It has to work for someone who has never bought online before.

Structure it like this:

  Part 1 — Who we are
    The workshop, the two brothers, the promise that nothing is resold. Keep it
    to two pages and use the site's own words.

  Part 2 — Choosing a bat
    The three ball types (soft, medium, hard) and why the ball decides the bat.
    Pickup weight, and what light, balanced and heavy feel like in the hands.
    Wood: Sri Lankan, Kashmir Willow, Poplar. Shapes: classic, scoop, flat face,
    big edge, short blade, layered blade. Playing styles and levels.
    Explain the colour coding used on every bat card: navy bars for weight, one
    to three, and orange bars for ball type, one to three.
    Then three ways to choose: browse the shop with filters; answer the Find My
    Bat questions; or ask on WhatsApp. Walk through each.

  Part 3 — Ordering
    Reading a bat's page. Engraving (₹199, up to 18 characters). Adding to the
    bag. Paying online by card, UPI or netbanking, or finishing the order on
    WhatsApp instead. What "Out of stock" means and what to do about it.
    Delivery, charges and free-delivery threshold — take the real numbers from
    the site. Tracking an order. Creating an account and what it gives you.

  Part 4 — Services
    One short chapter each: Bat Doctor repairs, custom bats, team jerseys,
    wholesale and club orders, corporate gifting, trade-in, and video content.
    For each: who it is for, what to send us, and what happens next.

  Part 5 — After you buy
    Looking after a tennis-ball bat. Warranty, returns and replacement, in plain
    English, matching the policy pages exactly. How to make a claim and what
    photographs to send. Refunds.

  Part 6 — Play & Win, and staying in touch
    The game and its discount codes. The WhatsApp line, the hours, and which
    number or email to use for what (Appendix B).

  Close with a one-page quick reference: contact details, hours, delivery
  timings, warranty in one line, and the website address.

DELIVERABLE
  Two separate documents, each with a cover page, a table of contents with page
  numbers, and numbered pages. Aim for 25–40 pages for Book 1 and 15–25 pages
  for Book 2 — long enough to be complete, short enough to be read. Finish with
  the screenshot checklist and a list of anything you marked [TO CONFIRM].

APPENDIX C — THE OUT-OF-STOCK SWITCH (newest feature, explain it in both books)
================================================================================
  In the Maze Room, Products, each bat's row has a button: "Mark out of stock"
  or "Mark in stock", and a column showing which it currently is. One click,
  no form to fill in.

  This is a switch, not a count. Bats are cut to order, so the stock number is
  often zero on purpose; the switch is how you say "do not sell this one now".

  What a customer then sees, straight away:
    - a dark OUT OF STOCK label on the bat's card and on its photo
    - the photo turns grey, and the bat moves to the bottom of its group so
      bats you can sell appear first
    - the bat's page still works and still appears in Google
    - Add to Bag and Buy now are replaced by "Ask when it is back", which opens
      WhatsApp
  An order containing that bat is refused by the database as well, so nobody
  can pay for something that cannot be shipped.
